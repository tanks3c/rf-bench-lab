Power conversion · Watts ↔ dBm

dBm is an absolute power level referenced to 1 milliwatt; dB is a ratio. A 3 dB increase is approximately double power.

Receiver signal level · dBm ↔ microvolts

Voltage represents an RMS sine-wave level across a matched impedance. Test equipment generator EMF/open-circuit voltage and loaded-terminal readings may differ by 6 dB. Do not confuse these with over-the-air field strength.

VSWR / reflected power

Ideal single-port math; does not locate the fault or account for measurement location, feedline attenuation, or instrument uncertainty. Transmit only into a verified rated load/system under approved procedures.

Feedline loss and radiated power

Use the actual cable datasheet loss at the operating frequency. ERP is referenced to a half-wave dipole; EIRP is referenced to isotropic. This estimate excludes mismatch and additional system losses unless entered.

Antenna length / electrical wavelength

Approximate starting lengths only. Final antenna dimensions depend on design, diameter, mounting, ground plane, and matching; tune/test with appropriate equipment. Coax electrical quarter-wave is not a whip antenna length.

12 V / 24 V DC voltage drop

Uses nominal solid-copper AWG resistance at 20 °C for full-length positive and return conductors. Stranded copper may run about 2–3% higher due to lay length; at 75 °C copper resistance is about 22% higher than at 20 °C (α ≈ 0.00393/°C). Check the actual cable datasheet, insulation, terminations, chassis return, and OEM limits. Voltage-drop math does NOT determine conductor ampacity or fuse size; use NEC Article 310, application requirements, and qualified installation procedures.

Battery / UPS runtime (rough)

Simple amp-hour estimate. Inverter efficiency, discharge curve, chemistry, age, temperature, generator/charger behavior, and minimum cutoff voltage may materially change actual runtime.

IPv4 subnet / backhaul calculator

Quick network math for radio-site switches and backhaul planning. The /31 and /32 cases are shown without assuming conventional host/broadcast use. This offline tool does not ping, scan, or connect to any network.

Repeater pair / offset

Offset = subscriber transmit frequency − subscriber receive frequency. Part 90 pairings are plan-specific; do not assume an amateur-band standard offset.

P25 NAC hex ↔ decimal

Network access code is 12-bit (0x000–0xFFF). This is a number-format converter, not a programming recommendation. Talkgroup IDs, radio IDs, and NACs are different fields.

Free-space path loss (FSPL)

Ideal unobstructed free space only. Distance units are converted before calculation (1 mi = 1.609344 km). Not a coverage forecast; terrain, buildings, foliage, diffraction, multipath, receiver performance, and system noise matter.

Mobile radio installation checklist

A general QA sequence for vehicle installations. Follow the radio maker's installation/RF exposure guide and the vehicle OEM upfitter documentation first.

Before drilling or routing: identify airbags/curtain deployment areas, wiring harnesses, fuel/brake systems and supplemental restraint components. De-energize and protect circuits as required by the vehicle and equipment manuals.

Common install symptoms

Radio reboots when PTT is pressed

Check input voltage at the radio under load, positive and return path voltage drop, fuse holder and terminals, wire gauge, grounds, and current demand. Evaluate with the OEM test procedure.

Alternator whine / ignition hash

Compare engine off/on. Check grounds, routing near vehicle electronics, accessory grounds, shield continuity, and approved suppression. Never bypass a protective fuse or airbag circuit.

Good receive, poor transmit range

Check RF output into a rated dummy load, then feedline losses, antenna match, connector damage, mount/ground plane, and transmit power setting. Consider receive site issues as well.

Unexpected PTT or accessory behavior

Check wiring/pinouts and accessory programming with vendor documentation; inspect for crushed cables and incompatible microphone/remote-head hardware.

Site / infrastructure maintenance checklist

Baseline what was working before changing anything. Use approved change control, isolation procedures, and site-specific acceptance limits.

Safety gate: Elevated work, live electrical service, RF exposure zones, generators/fuel systems, lightning protection, and energized batteries require qualified personnel and approved procedures. Coordinate RF shutdown and lockout/tagout when necessary. Do not climb a tower without required training, equipment, and authorization.

Common terms

PL / DPL, CTCSS / DCS

Analog access/squelch signaling. CTCSS uses a subaudible tone in Hz; DCS uses a coded digital sequence. A mismatch can keep the speaker muted despite an otherwise receivable signal. They are not encryption or private channels.

P25 NAC / TGID / RID

NAC is a 12-bit RF network access code. TGID is a talkgroup identifier; RID is a unit/subscriber identifier. Each serves a different purpose. A receive signal with the wrong NAC or talkgroup selection may not become user audio.

P25 Phase I vs Phase II

Phase I uses 12.5 kHz FDMA. Phase II trunked voice uses two-slot TDMA over 12.5 kHz channels; system compatibility depends on infrastructure, radio configuration, and deployment.

Conventional vs trunked

Conventional radios select a frequency/channel for a call. Trunked radios use a controller and control channel to assign traffic resources dynamically; affiliation and system permissions can matter.

RSSI / BER / SINAD / desense

RSSI indicates received signal strength; BER measures bit errors (digital); SINAD evaluates analog receiver sensitivity in a defined test; desensitization reduces receiver performance due to strong unwanted nearby RF. Compare measurements using an approved test setup and vendor criteria.

Duplexer / combiner / multicoupler

Duplexer isolates simultaneous transmitter/receiver paths; combiner allows multiple transmitters to share a transmission path; receiver multicoupler distributes signals to multiple receivers. Wrong alignment or poor isolation can create coverage issues.

Channel bandwidth and modulation

12.5-kHz narrowband-equivalent operation has been required for many Part 90 systems in 150–174 and 421–470 MHz since 2013, with exceptions/waivers. Licensed channel spacing, occupied bandwidth, and radio emission designator are distinct concepts. P25 Phase I is typically 12.5 kHz FDMA; P25 Phase II trunked voice uses two-slot TDMA.

LMR field instruments
  • Digital multimeter and DC clamp meter: source, ground, load current.
  • Service monitor: TX power, modulation, receiver sensitivity, BER/SINAD, signaling.
  • Directional wattmeter / calibrated power sensor: forward/reflected power.
  • VNA/antenna analyzer: VSWR, return loss, feedline fault isolation, sweep.
  • Rated dummy load/attenuators: bench RF testing without radiating.
  • Spectrum analyzer: interference/intermodulation and spurious signals.
  • Network tester / fiber meter: backhaul link validation as needed.

Check instrument range, calibration, safe input power, attenuation, and required test configuration before use. Never connect a direct high-power transmitter output to an unprotected analyzer.

End-to-end system troubleshooting order
  1. Confirm safety and outage scope with dispatch/control.
  2. Find comparison device/site/channel and reproduce symptom.
  3. Subscriber settings and authorized affiliation/access.
  4. DC power, RF antenna/coax and local interference.
  5. Repeater RF baseline, duplexer/combiner isolation.
  6. Network backhaul, control, timing, power/environment.
  7. Controlled retest and document before/after condition.

Technical basis and display policy

dBm = 10log10(P/1 mW); µV RMS = 10⁶√(P[W]·Z[Ω]); reflection coefficient |Γ| = (VSWR−1)/(VSWR+1); return loss = −20log10|Γ|; line loss in dB adds; EIRP = ERP + 2.15 dB for the ideal dipole reference; free-space attenuation = 32.44 + 20log10(f MHz) + 20log10(d km). DC drop assumes a copper circuit with supply + return; nominal coefficients are approximations. Results are estimates, not tolerance-bearing measurements or test certificates.
