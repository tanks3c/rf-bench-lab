Module 3 · Pool T9, T7

Feedlines and SWR

Coax has a characteristic impedance, Z0. When the load matches Z0, all forward power is absorbed. When it does not, some reflects back. Forward and reflected waves add into a standing wave. SWR is the ratio of its peak to its valley.

Γ = (ZL − Z0) ÷ (ZL + Z0)
SWR = (1 + |Γ|) ÷ (1 − |Γ|)
Reflected power = |Γ|²
Return loss = −20 × log10|Γ| dB
|Γ| at radio = |Γ| at load × 10^(−coax dB ÷ 10)

Antennas and the trim cycle

An antenna is resonant where its reactance crosses zero. Field techs sweep SWR across the band, read where the dip lands, trim, and sweep again. This bench simulates that sweep, the same view a NanoVNA or a Site Master gives you.

Module 5 · Pool T1, T2, T4

Repeaters and radio programming

A repeater listens on one frequency and transmits on another at the same time. Your radio has to transmit on the repeater's input, listen on its output, and send the right access tone. Getting those three fields right is most of radio programming, in ham and LMR alike.

Legal line. Until you hold a license, use the FT-60s to listen only. After you pass, transmit on amateur bands only. The FT-60 is an amateur radio, not a Part 90 LMR radio.

Link budgets and range

    
A link budget adds every gain and subtracts every loss between two radios. If the signal arrives above the receiver's sensitivity, the link works. Free-space loss is the floor. Terrain, buildings, and bodies only add to it.

    

    

      
FSPL (dB) = 20log10(d km) + 20log10(f MHz) + 32.44

      
RX = Ptx + Gtx − Ltx − FSPL − extra + Grx − Lrx

      
Horizon (km) ≈ 4.12 × (√h1 + √h2), h in m

      
LoRa sens ≈ −174 + 10log10(BW) + NF + SNRreq

Module 7 · Job track (not on the Tech exam)

    
LMR, P25, and PoC

    
Land mobile radio is the two-way radio that police, fire, utilities, and bases run on. Push-to-talk over cellular (your PoC radios) does a similar job over the phone network. The difference shows up when the network fails.

    

    

      
Where this meets cybersecurity

      
    
        
  • Encrypted P25 is only as good as its key handling. Keys load from a key variable loader or over the air (OTAR).
    • 
        
  • A 2011 University of Pennsylvania study found federal P25 users often sent sensitive traffic in the clear by mistake.
    • 
        
  • TETRA:BURST (2023) exposed a deliberately weakened cipher in the European TETRA standard.
    • 
        
  • Researchers found both with SDRs. That is your path from this lab to RF security.
